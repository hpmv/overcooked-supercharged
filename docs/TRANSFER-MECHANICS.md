# Carnival 3-4 transfer mechanics

Read-only audit of the installed native assembly, the original `s_day_3_4` bundle, and recorded gameplay artifacts. This document distinguishes source-supported interactions from completed live probes. It establishes no new route time or high-score result.

## What can travel together

| Object | Native carrying/transfer rule | Throw support in this level |
|---|---|---|
| Completed plated meal | One plate occupies the chef's single carried attachment; contents travel with it. | No. Initial plate10–13 and newly washed plate142 have no ThrowableItem/ClientThrowableItem. |
| Prepared empty bun | One carryable preparation container. | Native throwable callback allows only internal contents count zero. |
| Bun containing sausage, onion or sauce | Ingredients remain together inside that one bun; it can buffer a pot's cooked sausage before plating. | No. Adding any internal ingredient makes AllowThrowing false on client and server. |
| Pot, pan, mixing bowl, fryer basket | A vessel retains its contents during ordinary carrying; combining can pour contents while retaining the emptied vessel. | Recorded native cookware has no ThrowableItem. A mixed bowl cannot be thrown to the fryer. |
| Dirty plate stack | Ordinary pickup carries the entire stack. Compatible dirty stacks merge; depositing at the sink transfers the entire count and empties the chef's hands. | Returned dirty stack137 has no ThrowableItem. |
| Clean output stack | Picking up removes and carries exactly one clean plate. | No supported whole-stack pickup or clean-plate throw. |
| Loose ingredient | One ingredient per pickup; can be thrown and caught by a chef, appropriate vessel, or landed on an eligible surface. | Yes, subject to native callbacks, flight, collisions and destination restrictions. |

Throw eligibility is checked on the held object through IClientThrowable; use release does not turn a plate into a projectile. Plating changes ingredient composition, not the object's component capabilities. See [native throw input](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ClientPlayerControlsImpl_Default.cs:285>), [server preparation throw veto](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerPreparationContainer.cs:227>) and [client veto](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ClientPreparationContainer.cs:111>). Component evidence: [initial kitchen](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/kitchen-first.json>) (plates10–13, cookware2–9), [washed plate snapshot](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/throw-pot-c-state.json>) (plate142), and [dirty transfer trace](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/wash-cycle-a.jsonl.gz>) (frame1642, dirty stack137). No tray providing a multi-meal inventory was observed in the recorded kitchen.

## Harvest sausage first, add onion later

**Native rules permit a held, unplated prepared bun already containing cooked sausage to receive completed fried onion from a standing pan.** This is supported by code and serialized assets; the delayed-onion action itself still needs a successful live probe.

1. The [prepared bun prefab](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/downloadablecontent/dlc08/dlc_assets/prefabs/ingredients/HotdogBun_Chopped.prefab:174>) references the hotdog restrictions and has ingredient-container capacity **3** at line188. Its base bun is represented separately from those internal contents.
2. [HotdogPrefabLookup](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/downloadablecontent/dlc08/dlc_assets/data/orderdefinitions/DLC08_HotdogPrefabLookup.asset:14>) permits one BoiledFrankfurter (GUID `d0f59a99b37671b42984f7be42ee85b5`) and one FriedOnions (GUID `aa68af69a6c391e49bd8c9e3965da52c`), with no mutual restriction. FriedOnions is native cooked order node461172, not raw onion461162.
3. [ServerPlacementContainer.CanHandlePlacement](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerPlacementContainer.cs:29>) explicitly checks a preparation container held by a player against the standing target's order composition. Its HandlePlacement reverse-transfer branch then pours the pan's native contents into the held bun's ingredient container. The bun remains held, and the pan becomes empty.
4. [CanAddOrderContents](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerPreparationContainer.cs:146>) applies both the composition restriction and container capacity; [CompositeAssembledNode](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/CompositeAssembledNode.cs:170>) enforces per-ingredient limits. [AssembledNodeTransfer](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/AssembledNodeTransfer.cs:188>) also prevents transfer midway through cooking. Use the completed, unburnt fried-onion node; duplicate onions and incompatible states are rejected.

The primitive should reserve the exact bun and pan; require the same bun held afterward, one new cooked onion in that bun, unchanged sausage content, and an empty pan. It must not treat a generic held-item change as successful assembly. Carrying the cooked pan to a standing bun is also handled natively, but then the chef retains the emptied pan ([preparation placement](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerPreparationContainer.cs:192>)).

This enables pot turnover before onion preparation or clean plates become available. It does not make the resulting hotdog throwable.

## Raw throws: receiver rules and proof

[ServerIngredientCatcher](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerIngredientCatcher.cs:20>) checks the object's AllowCatch callback, attachment requirement when configured, target callback, nonempty order composition, legal container transfer, and available ingredient capacity. On success it transfers the food, informs native contents change, and destroys the original loose ingredient.

The live [first pot attempt](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/throw-pot-c.jsonl.gz>) demonstrates interception: Frankfurter144 was aimed at pot2 but nearer pot7 caught it. [second pot attempt](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/throw-second-pot.jsonl.gz>) then succeeded into pot2 with the nearer pot full. Therefore aim coordinates and source disappearance cannot certify the requested receiver. Reserve one destination and prove its expected food delta from the original ingredient, while detecting another vessel's interception.

An empty ordinary counter follows a different rule: [ServerAttachStation.CanHandleCatch](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerAttachStation.cs:450>) requires `m_canCatch` and a throwable that **is no longer flying** before attaching it. An occupied station delegates to its occupant's catcher. [ServerAttachmentCatchingProxy](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerAttachmentCatchingProxy.cs:39>) examines actual unattached objects in recorded trigger collisions. A counter route should prove the intended surface's attachment ID after native landing; entering its XY/XZ bounds midflight is insufficient. The planner's far-bowl throw landing on an intervening board is existing evidence of that distinction.

A pipeline can release the thrower's reservation after native held-item release while an independent arrival observer tracks the original entity/thrower and reserved receiver. That is a source-supported scheduling opportunity; the current blocking throw action waits for arrival. No throughput saving is asserted until the pipelined route is measured.

## Dirty batches and clean handoffs

[ServerDirtyPlateStack](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerDirtyPlateStack.cs:28>) merges same-plating-step stacks by transferring their count and destroying the held source. It has no special one-plate pickup implementation, so the carryable stack moves as a whole. [ServerWashingStation.HandlePlacement](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerWashingStation.cs:177>) destroys the carried dirty-stack object and adds its entire stack count to the sink. A center chef can consequently carry several returned dirty plates across one shared-counter handoff.

Washing still processes individual plates. [native sink update](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerWashingStation.cs:83>) advances only while natively interacting, finishes when progress is strictly greater than the configured time, resets the timer, decrements dirty count by one and returns one clean plate. Original bundle objects8729/8730 on WashingPart game object759 specify multiple interactors enabled and a **3.0-second** clean-plate time. The exported [sink prefab](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/downloadablecontent/dlc08/dlc_assets/prefabs/shared kitchen/DLC08_workstation_01_sink_circus.prefab:294>) has the same duration and `m_allowMultipleInteracters: 1` at line314. Native progress is multiplied by interactor count when enabled. A second washer is legal, but the required travel, lower-left access, and displaced job time are unmeasured.

[ServerCleanPlateStack.HandlePickup](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerCleanPlateStack.cs:77>) removes only one plate. Its placement handler delegates food placement to the top plate; it does not merge clean plates. [ServerPlateReturnStation](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerPlateReturnStation.cs:57>) rejects player placement into the return station. Keep the drying output clear of objects other than its native clean stack; the sink suppresses interaction for incompatible drying occupants.

Dirty stacks also have the separate Washable/water-spray pathway that can replace an entire stack with a clean stack. This is not the sink deposit pathway, and no usable water gun was observed in this level. It is not an established shortcut for carrying clean batches. [ServerWashable](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/ServerWashable.cs:149>) requires a registered washing source, such as ServerWaterGunSpray; holding use by itself does not wash a carried stack.

Plate return is delayed natively: [PlateReturnController](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/Scripts/Assembly-CSharp/PlateReturnController.cs:67>) queues each delivered plate, decrements its timer with native delta and returns it only after expiry and an available return station. [Day_3_4_4P](<M:/projects/AssetRipper/Source/0Bins/AssetRipper.Tools.SystemTester/Release/Ripped/ExportedProject/Assets/downloadablecontent/dlc08/dlc_assets/data/levelconfigs/Day_3_4_4P.asset:23>) specifies a 7-second return delay. Batch admission must account for plates in meals, travel, pending return, dirty stacks, sink, drying output and clean handoffs; waiting for every plate before beginning a batch can starve the next meal.

## Concrete service layout

The validated optimization direction is **several one-plate deliveries during one lower-right visit**, supplied through shared counters. It is not multiple plates carried through one cannon shot.

Positions and IDs from [cycle-start snapshot](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/cycle-start.json>):

| Link | Native stations | Scheduling use |
|---|---|---|
| Center → lower-right server | Counters52/47/49 at X25.2, Z−18/−19.2/−20.4 | Reserve individual plates and slots; center refills a vacated slot while server travels to delivery73 at (32.4,−19.8). |
| Center ↔ lower-left washer | Counters50/46/44 at X15.6, Z−18/−19.2/−20.4 | Carry dirty stacks outward; hand clean plates inward one at a time. |
| Dirty return → sink | Return74 at (20.4,−21.6); sink75 at (13.207,−21.6), output76 at (12.005,−21.6) | Batch dirty travel, start partial washing before clean inventory is exhausted, and prioritize clearing clean output. |

Counter pickup/placement, one-meal cannon delivery/portal return, a one-plate wash cycle and raw vessel catching have live evidence. The complete repeated multi-delivery wave, delayed-onion harvest and multiple-interactor wash have not yet been demonstrated as one successful route. See [measured action scope and timings](<M:/projects/game-test-2/docs/THROUGHPUT.md>); the 5.367-second single-meal loop cannot simply be multiplied into a complete scheduling proof.

## Collision details for transfer navigation

Do not globally discard attached cookware collisions. The original PhysicsManager matrix ([decoded engine settings](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/native-engine-settings.json>), corroborated directly against original globalgamemanagers) enables **Players8 ↔ Attachments14**, Players8 ↔ HeldAttachments15, and Players8 ↔ Walls10/Worktops11. HeldAttachments15 ↔ Attachments14 is disabled. Ground is layer9, not the chef layer.

[planner-four-a blocked snapshot](<M:/projects/game-test-2/artifacts/planner-four-a-state.json>) exposes a specific bounding-box false positive: chef106 at (23.688467,−11.7999954), capsule radius0.4, is 0.50958 from bowl6's actual yaw-rotated 0.7×0.7 box. Its world AABB overlaps the expanded path obstacle by about0.03714, but the oriented shape remains clear by about0.10958. Resolve this with oriented narrow-phase geometry or a validated outward departure, not by removing all layer14 obstacles. Carried plates can still collide with walls/worktops as compound attachments, so replayed cannon approaches must retain the observed open-floor staging.
